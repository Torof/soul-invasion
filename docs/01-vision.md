# 01 — Vision

## The concept in one sentence

An online game where every player is a **parasite soul** that incarnates into the inhabitants of a living world, lives a whole life, dies, then is reborn elsewhere, carrying its knowledge across the centuries of a civilization the players build themselves, from the Stone Age to wherever they choose to take it.

## The three pillars

1. **Build.** Everything is done by hand, slowly, concretely: fell the tree, knap the stone, assemble the axe, raise the house.
2. **Live in society.** Politics, economy, alliances, betrayals, religions: all of it is created by players and NPCs.
3. **Live, die, be reborn.** Death is frequent and sudden. Living well, dying usefully and being reborn wisely is a skill in its own right.

## Game philosophy

- **Slow, immersive, realistic.** The opposite of games where a building appears in two seconds. An army is built with people, tools and food, not with a timer.
- **Work counts, intelligence multiplies.** Repetitive work is linear; tools, organization, knowledge, enterprise and reading the markets are multipliers. In the Stone Age, muscle matters; the further civilization advances, the more organization wins.
- **The game guarantees nothing.** Everything is physical and social. Nothing is protected by magic. A law only exists if someone enforces it. Theft, black markets, corruption, police, investigations: everything is possible.
- **Nothing magical in trade.** Goods travel physically (convoys, carts, horses, drivers, escorts, bandits). Markets are local. Information itself travels physically: a shout, a messenger, a rumor.
- **Knowledge is a resource.** It is kept, sold, stolen, protected. What truly survives death is what the player knows.
- **Structures outlive their founders.** Guilds, empires, brotherhoods, merchant congresses carry on after their creator's death, taken over by a player or an NPC.
- **Playing alone is possible, but nearly impossible to sustain.** Wild beasts, raiders, slavers: solitude is a danger.

## Goals

The game offers goals on several time scales:

- **Immediate**: survive the night, craft your first axe, get accepted by the tribe.
- **One life**: found a business, become chief, build a city, prepare an heir.
- **Several lives**: elevate your soul, reach ranks, unlock powers.
- **Collective**: build wonders, bring your civilization into a new era, protect the world (or destroy it).

There is **no final summit**, but there are **limited seats** at the top: eight Demon Lords, a single World Emperor. You rise by taking someone's seat; the difficulty comes from the other souls, and death always ends up freeing the seats. It is a permanent loop.

## Audience

The game is aimed at an **adult audience**: betrayals, slavery, inquisition, spiritual wars, dark rituals. These themes are handled in a suggestive rather than graphic way.

## Build charter

- **Depth before size.** Three incredible villages are worth more than an empty continent.
- **Do little, but do it very well.** Everything that gets built must be great.
- **We dare not to build.** Giving up on a feature is a valid decision.
- **Ship early and often.** As soon as a system is fun, it goes out; we carry on with the players.
- **Fun first.** A passion project, participatory, faithful to its creator's values. No race for millions.
- **No brutal wipe.** If a new world opens, souls can migrate to it; the old world stays alive.
- **The full vision is a north star, not a plan.** Every step must be playable and fun in itself.

## Inspirations

EVE Online (single world, metagame, espionage), Haven & Hearth, Albion (gathering, local markets), Crusader Kings (dynasties, characters), Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld (NPCs and emergent stories), OGame (alliances and roles), reincarnation and cultivation novels (a soul that rises, the Aura of Destiny).

Also worth remembering: **Chronicles of Elyria**, a project with similar ideas that collapsed without shipping, for lack of advancing through playable steps.
