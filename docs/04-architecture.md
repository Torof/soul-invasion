# 04 — Architecture Decisions

Each decision is recorded with its **reason** and its **consequences**. These are the foundations: getting them wrong would cost rewrites.

---

### D01 — Platform: PC first
- **Reason**: rich interface (chats, maps, management), online community, simple distribution (Steam, early access), freedom to update. Consoles: access barrier (dev kits, certifications); mobile: screen too small, wrong audience.
- **Consequences**: also playable with a controller from the start (Steam Deck, a Switch port possible one day).

### D02 — Visual style: isometric
- **Reason**: readability of towns, roads, constructions and armies; a natural grid for building.
- **Consequences**: choice between pure 2D and light 3D with a fixed camera and painted rendering, to be settled according to the graphics solution (probably AI). Visual consistency is the main challenge.
- **Settled on 6 November 2026: pure 2D isometric**, a 2:1 diamond grid of one-metre cells (64 × 32 px at scale 1), the ground from top-down textures projected and blended by the engine, elements as flat images standing on it, relief later as whole levels. A relief ground in 3D was weighed and declined: it buys continuous hills and costs the grid everything else rests on. The format every image obeys is in `09-visual-production.md`; the style in `07-style-bible.md`.

### D03 — Client: Godot (GDScript)
- **Reason**: free under the MIT license with no revenue condition, suited to 2D and stylized 3D, mature, readable text files (efficient with AI).
- **Consequences**: the client only **displays** and **transmits the player's intentions**.

### D04 — Simulation server: Rust, custom-built
- **Reason**: performance for a continuous simulation of tens of thousands of entities; a strict compiler that acts as a guardrail for AI-written code; total control (accelerated time, granularity). SpacetimeDB ruled out: non-free license, designed for queries rather than a heavy continuous simulation.
- **Consequences**: two languages at the heart of the project. Side services (accounts, website, tools) possibly in TypeScript.

### D05 — The server is the only source of truth
- **Reason**: anti-cheat, a world that lives without connected players.
- **Consequences**: the client decides nothing. The hidden rules (the Emperor's tree, karma) exist **only on the server**, never in the installed game files.

### D06 — Simulation separated from display, from day one
- **Reason**: going online must never require a rewrite.
- **Consequences**: even the first version runs as a Godot client connected to a local Rust server.

### D07 — A single persistent world, divided into regions
- **Reason**: wars, convoys and politics require a continuous world; locality (like EVE's solar systems) allows the load to be spread.
- **Consequences**: at first, everything fits on one machine; the region structure allows spreading later. If the population demands it, we open **another world** rather than expanding indefinitely.

### D08 — Interest management
- **Reason**: each player only receives what happens around them (performance, network).
- **Consequences**: reinforces the principle that information travels physically.
- **Status** (5 November 2026, step 1b): built at chunk granularity. Each connection holds a view of 19×19 chunks (608 m across, 9 November 2026; it was 5×5, then 9×9, then 11×11, then 13×13, then 15×15: the faster generation and loading of the performance audit paid for it, about 11 s of loading without a window, 480 MB on the client, the frames unchanged since only what is on screen is drawn) around its body. The client's view reaches **288 tiles from the body** either way, as far as those chunks always reach, and moves with it **tile by tile** (the chunks held move by whole chunks, the view does not: the lead's call, smooth and natural). Its edge is a fog in three parts, named by the lead so they can be tuned by name (`client/world/world_view.gd`), from the inside out: **fog C**, 15 tiles where a veil of the fog's own colour starts to rise over the **ground only**, what stands (trees, plants, items, bodies) showing over it; **fog B**, the view's last 35 tiles, where the veil keeps rising over **everything**, whole at the limit; **fog A**, 5 tiles of fog past the limit with nothing drawn under it, in soft drifting banks. The fog is a soft grey (a white one was too loud) and 70 % thick at most, so the land past the limit is still guessed through it; the land keeps its colours and melts into it. The view's corners are rounded (64 tiles at the limit, the inner fogs' arcs sharing their centre): a square corner looked odd. The **camera stops** at fog A's end: no corner of the screen passes it, so pushing to an edge shows the fogs and never what lies beyond, and walking that way moves them along (the lead's calls, 9 November 2026, tried live: a ring of 2 outline chunks fading over 64 tiles showed too much; 3 tiles blurring past the chunks held with the fog barely seen did not tell where the view ends; darkening the land under the veil was not what was meant; the veil on the ground only let what stands show over it, which the lead liked; 3/6/20 tiles of fog and 3/10/20/30 of veil were tried before 3, 9 and 15, then 20, 20 and 15, then 5, 35 and 15; the ring of outline chunks, protocol v15 `chunk_outline`, is gone in v16, so the client holds only the 19 × 19 chunks). **The view of the land and the range of the creatures are to be two distances** (the lead agreed, 9 November 2026): the land, still and cheap once loaded, is sent out to the view; the creatures (animals, people, players) that move only within what the body perceives (D18), a smaller range, so that many of them do not cost each client in proportion to the view's area. Under the fog the client still draws the **far view** (protocol v14, `far_view`): the land 2 km around the body, one letter a cell of 8 tiles (grass, forest, water, rock), sent after the first chunks and again when the body has gone 16 chunks from its centre, drawn under the chunks with a grain; the fog hides it now, and whether it stays is to be decided. The connection receives those chunks with the bodies in them, then only the moves inside them; as the body walks, chunks entering the view are sent and chunks leaving it are hidden, and a body walking out is reported as left. Perception by line of sight, hearing and witnesses (D18) will refine this inside the view.

### D09 — Adjustable time and a calendar specific to each world
- **Reason**: the world has its own calendar and its own biology; testing requires massive acceleration.
- **Consequences**: durations (day, season, childhood, lifetime) are adjustable independently and defined as world data. An accelerated mode without players is **mandatory** from the start.

### D10 — Levels of detail in the simulation
- **Reason**: simulating everything in fine detail everywhere is impossible.
- **Consequences**: full detail near players, summarized simulation elsewhere, bulk simulation far away, with a return to the individual level when a player arrives. States (memories, opinions) are never lost.

### D11 — Target: several tens of thousands of NPCs
- **Reason**: a living, populated world.
- **Consequences**: data organized for performance; thinking **spread over time**; optimized movement (precomputed paths, no pathfinding for distant NPCs); **relationships limited to known people** (~150); old memories summarized.

### D12 — Willpower sets the richness of NPC thought
- **Reason**: lore, reincarnation cost and performance brought together in a single notion.
- **Consequences**: Willpower sets **how far** an NPC thinks; proximity to players sets **how often**.

### D13 — A single type of inhabitant; the soul is a pilot
- **Reason**: reincarnation must feel natural.
- **Consequences**: no code difference between player and NPC at the body level. A body is piloted either by the AI or by a soul.

### D14 — Souls live above the worlds
- **Reason**: soul migrations, multiple worlds, the possible end of a world.
- **Consequences**: soul data is stored in a separate service, independent of any world.

### D15 — All content as data
- **Reason**: technologies without an imposed tree, invention by assembly, evolution without recoding.
- **Consequences**: materials with properties, parts, processes, composite objects whose qualities are **computed**. Never a recipe hard-coded in the code.
- **Status** (5 November 2026, milestone 2): first content files, `content/materials.ron` (family, mass, hardness, flammability, food value, provisional colour) and `content/spawns.ron` (what lies on which terrain, in pieces per thousand tiles). The valley is scattered from them when a world is created; items are world state, in the snapshot (format v4). Parts, processes and computed qualities come with crafting.
- **Amended on 10 November 2026 — the ground's items made per chunk.** What the ground itself drops (dead wood under the trees, pebbles by the water: the only spawn rules left, `content/spawns.ron`) is made per chunk from the seed and the rules, each tile and rule rolling its own hashed chance, and never stored (`sim::items`, D31). An item has a fixed id from its tile and rule (below 2^53, so a client reading JSON numbers as floats keeps it exact). Only what a body touches becomes a stored item; the natural ones taken are remembered, so they do not come back. Nothing ready-made lies about any more: berries, fibres and flint will come from the bushes, gathered plants and the rock (the project lead: players gather raw matter and learn how to make things). The 3072 valley's snapshot fell from 18.4 MB (216,186 stored items) to under a kilobyte. Snapshot format v8: worlds made before keep their scattered items, all stored, and make no natural ones.

### D16 — Object states computed on demand
- **Reason**: spoilage, wear, preservation for millions of objects.
- **Consequences**: the state is computed at the moment it is consulted, from the date and the history (smoking, cold…), not updated continuously.

### D17 — Permanent event journal
- **Reason**: the world's memory, historical merit, investigations, karma, NPC memories.
- **Consequences**: significant events kept forever; fine details kept temporarily. Volume to be monitored.
- **Amended on 10 November 2026**: "significant" now means what lasts: a death, an item made or gone (D28). A soul's deeds will be kept in the souls' own store only until they unlock a title, a power or a skill, then folded into the soul's state (running totals such as karma kept as totals) and erased: history is a means to unlock, not an archive.

### D18 — Witnesses and perception
- **Reason**: crimes, investigations, rumors, suspicion, justice.
- **Consequences**: every action has potential witnesses; every witness keeps a memory of it. No omniscient information.

### D19 — Generic group system
- **Reason**: all of society (tribe, guild, religion, empire) rests on it.
- **Consequences**: members, roles, powers, rules, succession, hierarchy, relations, visibility. The tribe of the first version is already a group. Role powers are **social recognitions**, not locks.

### D20 — Interactions through intentions
- **Reason**: to allow generated speech and free conversation later without rebuilding anything.
- **Consequences**: menus, gestures or free sentences all translate into intentions handled the same way by the NPC's brain.

### D21 — Versioned data
- **Reason**: to evolve the world and migrate souls without breaking everything.
- **Consequences**: every save format is versioned and migratable from the start.

### D22 — Evolving security and balance
- **Reason**: like an ecosystem, balance is built through iterations.
- **Consequences**: no large initial anti-cheat system beyond server authority; we observe, we adjust.

*Technical skeleton decisions, approved on 5 November 2026.*

### D23 — A single repository (monorepo)
- **Reason**: client, server, content and documents evolve together; one version, one history, one CI.
- **Consequences**: structure `docs/` (this corpus), `server/` (Rust), `client/` (Godot), `content/` (world data).

### D24 — The simulation is a pure library, with no network and no clock
- **Reason**: it is the most important piece (NPCs, Willpower, needs) and the hardest to get right. Isolated, it is tested in milliseconds, runs in accelerated mode without a client (D09) and depends on no network technology.
- **Consequences**: three Rust crates from the start. `sim`: the world, the NPCs, the rules; no input/output; receives intentions and a number of ticks, returns events. `protocol`: the client ↔ server message types, in a single place. `server`: network, persistence, time loop; the only crate that talks to the outside world. **Fixed tick** (order of magnitude: 10 ticks per second of game time); acceleration only changes the tick rate, never the rules. Same inputs, same result: essential for tests and for replaying a bug.

- **Amended on 9 November 2026 — world generation is integer only**: a seed must give the same world on every machine and every build, since a world is regenerated, not stored, and keeps its generator for life. So generation uses integers only, its building blocks (the hash, the noises, the random streams) live in one module, `sim::gen`, with golden values pinned by tests, and floats are linted across the `sim` crate (`clippy::float_arithmetic` and a list of float functions in `server/sim/clippy.toml`, errors under `-D warnings`). The floats still there carry a commented exception: those in generation go when the generator is rewritten (the world by chunk); those that move bodies or tell the time are not generation and stay.

### D25 — Transport: WebSocket
- **Reason**: native in Godot 4 (`WebSocketPeer`), mature in Rust (`tokio-tungstenite`). Goes through everywhere (firewalls, proxies), trivial TLS later, opens the door to a web client and to administration tools in a browser. The game is slow: no need for UDP.
- **Consequences**: no high-level Godot multiplayer API. The client has a single connection, a single network module.

### D26 — Message encoding: JSON, behind a single door
- **Reason**: readable in logs, parsed natively by Godot, trivial to debug. The volumes of the first version are small.
- **Consequences**: encoding is confined to a single module on each side, so we can switch to binary the day it matters without touching the rest. Every message carries a type and a protocol version. The Rust types in `protocol` are the reference; the client mirrors them. **Shape of the dialogue** (D05, D08, D20): client → server, intentions only; server → client, an initial state of what the body perceives, then changes. The client never receives the whole world: the ground travels in chunks around the body (D08, D31).

### D27 — Content in RON, read by the server only
- **Reason**: content (D15) is made of nested structures and categories (materials, parts, processes). RON is Rust's native data format: it expresses them directly, accepts comments and is checked at load time. The client does not need to read it: it receives the names and visual identifiers from the server at connection.
- **Consequences**: hidden rules stay on the server effortlessly (D05). Content is validated at server startup: an inconsistent file prevents launch; a CI test loads all the content at every commit.
- **Status** (5 November 2026, milestone 2): built. `sim::content` parses and validates (unique `snake_case` ids, resolved references, sane values); `server::content` is the only place files are read, from `--content <DIR>` or the first of `content`, `../content`, `../../content`. The server prints what it loaded at startup.

### D28 — Persistence: event journal + snapshots, SQLite first
- **Reason**: the simulation lives in memory; we persist the event journal (D17) and periodic snapshots. SQLite is a single file, with no server to install, sufficient for a tribe and a valley.
- **Consequences**: storage goes through a single interface (`Storage`); replacing it with PostgreSQL only touches that module. Schema and snapshots versioned with migrations from day one (D21). Soul data lives in a separate file, behind its own interface (D14).
- **Status** (milestone 1 audit, 5 November 2026): schema v1, snapshot format v2 (calendar, tick, map, bodies), one snapshot per game day plus the journal. Nothing is pruned yet; the retention policy is an open question (`06`).
- **Amended on 10 November 2026 — first storage diet** (the lightness audit, `content/ai-reserve/review/lightness-audit/`): the journal keeps only what lasts (a death, an item made or gone); steps, gauges, what is held and refusals are the state of the moment and live in the snapshots. Journaling them cost about 400 MB a real day with 100 players, for a journal nothing reads back. Only the **last three snapshots** are kept (each was a full copy of the world, 18 MB a game day, never pruned). A snapshot writes the map as its spec straight from the map in place, no longer copying every tile first (57 MB on the 3072 valley): 13 ms instead of 22 ms per snapshot on that valley, the same bytes. Losing up to a few minutes of play on a crash is accepted by the project lead, which opens the way to the target design: current state only, written in the background, souls and their deeds in their own file (D14), deeds kept only until they unlock a title, power or skill.

### D29 — One server, one simulation thread
- **Reason**: the simulation is deterministic and sequential, the network is asynchronous; mixing them creates the hardest bugs there are.
- **Consequences**: the simulation loop runs on a single thread and receives intentions through a queue; the network (tokio) runs alongside and only translates. The split into regions (D07) will come later with several loops of this kind.
- **Precision** (milestone 1 audit, 5 November 2026): the queue holds **one pending intention per body**, the latest wins, taken before each tick. A client flooding `move_to` costs one path search per tick and nothing to anyone else. Refusals go back to the pilot alone; moves are published to all. The listener also bounds what the outside can cost: a handshake deadline, a maximum message size, a maximum number of connections (`--max-connections`).

### D30 — Public repository on GitHub (`Torof`), code under AGPL-3.0, lore and documents under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
- **Reason**: a participatory project, open to ideas and new people. Two protections are wanted: nobody must be able to appropriate the project to turn it into a closed commercial product, nor take it "their own way" under the same name. The AGPL forces anyone who runs the code (even as an online service) to publish their modifications; CC BY-NC-SA forbids commercial use of the world, the lore and the documents, and requires share-alike. The game's name and its identity remain the property of the project lead.
- **Consequences**: the hidden rules (karma, the Emperor's tree, titles) are **content**, in a second **private** repository loaded by the production server; the public repository contains dummy values so that everyone can compile and play. This private repository is created the day the first hidden rule appears. The project lead keeps the final say on what goes into the project (`CONTRIBUTING.md`). Contributions are accepted under the same licenses.

### D31 — Spatial scale: one tile is about one metre, on a single continuous grid
- **Reason**: cities must be able to grow from one hut to walled towns built by players, and the player must enter a house and see its rooms. That needs the tile to be the smallest thing that takes room: a body, a door, a wall segment, a bed, a fire. A hut is then 3×3 tiles, a house with rooms 6×8 to 10×12, a rampart one tile thick and as long as its builders' patience. Interiors stay on the same grid rather than opening a separate map: a house is walls on tiles, rooms are what walls enclose, the roof is hidden when a watched body is inside. This keeps everything physical: fire spreads, sound carries, witnesses see through a door (D18), a stone rampart is as many demolition sites as it has segments, and no system needs a special case for "door". Separate maps cost the same storage and bandwidth (the tiles exist either way); they save rendering complexity and lose coherence. A region (D07) is what a separate map is for: caves, other worlds — not houses.
- **Consequences**: the first valley is 3072×3072 tiles (about thirty-six minutes of walking across; 8 November 2026, it was 256×256, then 768×768 and 1536×1536 the same day). It is meant to grow tenfold, a hundredfold and more: nothing on the client or the server may cost in proportion to the whole map rather than to the view around a body. A walking step takes about 0.7 s. The grid is cut into **chunks of 32×32 tiles**, the unit of storage, of transfer to clients and of interest (D08): a client receives the chunks around its body. Terrain is **generated from parameters** and never stored tile by tile; what changes the world is objects placed on tiles, through the journal (D17). One level only for the first version; floors are an open question (`06`). Rendering draws only the visible chunks; the client needs a camera (zoom, pan). A path search explores at worst all the ground a body can reach, which on 3072×3072 tiles is already slow for a target across a river without a ford; hierarchical paths come with many NPCs (D10, D11) and will be needed sooner as the map grows.
- **Amended on 10 November 2026 — a body takes the ways it sees, otherwise it walks straight** (stage 0 of the world-by-chunk proposal): a path search stays within what the body sees, a square of **288 tiles on each side** (`PATH_REACH`, the view, D08). A ford, a bridge, a way round a pond in sight is taken, exactly as the whole-map search found it; nothing beyond sight is ever searched, so the body never finds by magic a way the player has not seen. When there is no such way, the body is not refused: it **walks straight toward the target and stops before the first tile it may not enter**, and the player finds the way round (the land is knowledge, `03` §11: no map is shown). So no search explores more than 577 × 577 tiles, about 50 to 100 ms at worst in a release build, whatever the size of the world, where a target across a river with no ford on the 3072 valley used to explore its whole bank, 1.9 million tiles. The search's memory follows the chunks it touches, never the map's size. Planned, not built: re-planning with what comes into sight as the body walks, waypoints the player sets, and sight hidden by groves, rocks and hills (with perception, D18); NPCs and animals will get their own way of moving (D10, D11). A first plan, a budget of expanded tiles with legs toward the closest tile reached, was dropped as more complex than a game where each player steers one body needs.

### D32 — Walking in eight directions, with weighted steps
- **Reason**: with only four directions every oblique walk is a staircase, visible at one metre per tile and worse once animals flee and hunters chase. The movement model is a foundation for routines, hunting and carrying: changing it later means retesting all of them.
- **Consequences**: paths use A* over eight neighbours; a diagonal step costs √2 straight ones (10 and 14 in path units) and is never allowed to cut a corner between two impassable tiles. Ties break deterministically.
- **Amended on 5 November 2026 — fine positions and taut paths**: eight directions still make every oblique walk a staircase, and finer grids only make finer staircases. So a body stands at a **fine position** (millimetres) inside the one-metre grid, which stays the unit of everything else (items, buildings, occupation, perception, the eight-direction facing of combat). A walk is planned on the grid, **weighed by the content's cost of each terrain** (`content/movement.ron`: a road is cheap, a forest dear, water impossible), then **pulled taut** into straight segments that cross only tiles no dearer than those they replace — so a road is taken when it is worth the detour and never short-cut through the field beside it. The body advances along the segments every tick at the terrain's speed; it crosses tiles and stops exactly on the target's centre. Clients receive the route once (`body_walks`) and interpolate, instead of one message per metre. Paths cost the same as before; hierarchical paths still come with many NPCs. (10 November 2026: a walk with no path in sight goes straight toward its target until something stands in the way, D31. A body that a straight segment leaves a few millimetres into a tile it may not enter, grazing its corner, walks on at the slowest pace instead of standing stuck.)

### D33 — Combat: slow real time by intentions, exchanges at a tempo, wounds instead of hit points
- **Reason**: this is a game of lives and consequences, not a fighting game. Combat must be deliberate and rare, quick and lethal, heavy with consequences (witnesses, opinions, karma), and the same system for everything that hurts: a predator, a fall, an assassin. It must also be *played* between two players who think and react, and scale to a battle of a hundred soldiers.
- **Consequences**:
  - **Tempo**: an engaged body commits one **gesture** per tempo (about a game second, slowed by fatigue and wounds). Between tempos the player chooses the next gesture; with no choice, the body follows its **guard**. Players are not asked for reflexes but for reading and choice; network latency vanishes behind a second.
  - **Gestures and guards**: strike, feint, parry, dodge, shove, break off — each with a direction and, for strikes, a target limb; three guards (offensive, closed, evasive). They answer each other like a game of reading (a feint beats a parry, a shove beats a parry, a dodge beats a shove), with uncertainty from skill, condition and equipment.
  - **Simultaneous resolution, moves hidden**: the gestures of every engaged body resolve together at the tempo. One sees the opponent's guard and, during the tempo, the **wind-up** of the gesture — late and ambiguous from a skilled fighter, plain from a novice; a feint shows a false wind-up. All of it is computed in the simulation from the content (D15): the weapon's reach, mass, edge and balance from its parts; the protection worn by limb; the body's skill with that family of weapon, learnt by practice; its condition (fatigue, wounds by limb); its facing (eight directions, D32), the measure (reach strikes first as distance closes), the ground; and the number of attackers, each beyond the first taking from the defence.
  - **No hit points**: a hit gives a wound to a limb (light, grave, mortal) that bleeds, hampers, heals over days and scars. Vitality (D34) is what hunger, cold, blood loss and fatigue drain. Death comes from a mortal wound or from vitality at zero. A dead body is a corpse: an item on the ground, like an animal's.
  - **Assassination is combat against someone who does not know**: asleep, from behind, in the dark, or trusting — no parry, a first blow almost always mortal. Perception (D18) and awareness decide it.
  - **War is the same system**: a hundred engaged bodies are a hundred resolutions per game second. Lines, flanking and breaches emerge from positions and facing; orders are group intentions (D19); morale (Willpower, fear, seeing neighbours fall) makes bodies break and run; fatigue wears a line down. There is no battle mode and no arena instance: an arena is a place.
  - **The engagement window** on the client is a *view* of the same world, not an instance: the camera zooms on the engaged bodies and a panel shows both silhouettes — the opponent's is the attack command (click where you strike), one's own the defence command — while the map stays visible around it, so one sees who comes from behind and can break off and flee across it. Commands are **composable by the player** from the full grammar the server accepts (a default panel of a few, more granular ones to add, keys to assign), but **never conditional**: one intention per tempo, no "if he strikes left, parry left" — that is an NPC brain, and a player who has one is a bot. NPCs use exactly the same grammar.
  - Built with the animals (milestone 2), tried against the predator and in the hunt; witnesses, morale and orders with milestone 4. Tempo length, the gesture set and every number are content.

### D34 — The state of a body: vitality, needs, wounds by limb
- **Reason**: needs, combat, animals, illness and aging all act on the same body and must agree on what hurts and what kills, before any of them is built.
- **Consequences**: a body has a **vitality** (0–100), three **needs** (hunger, cold, fatigue, 0–100), a flag for sleep, and **wounds by limb** (head, torso, arms, legs: none, light, grave, mortal; bleeding or not). Needs rise with time and fall with what relieves them (food, warmth, sleep); at their maximum they drain vitality; a full fatigue forces sleep. Vitality regains slowly when nothing drains it. Death comes at zero vitality or from a mortal wound; the body becomes a corpse item and its pilot is told. Rates and thresholds are content (`content/needs.ron`). Wounds (D33) reduce what a limb can do; until combat exists they are carried empty. Clients see only their own body's gauges.
- **Death and reattachment, provisionally**: until milestone 5 brings the choice of body and rebirth, a pilot whose body dies takes the first free body, or watches if none is free.

### D35 — Objects and carrying: one place at a time, a hand and a load in kilograms
- **Reason**: picking up, carrying, laying down, eating, crafting, fighting and trading all move objects between bodies and the ground; they must agree on where an object is and what a body can hold, before any of them is built. Tools and weapons are *held*: what is in a body's hand is seen by all.
- **Consequences**: an item is in **exactly one place** at a time — on a tile, carried by a body, or (later) in a container or a structure; never in two, never nowhere. A body carries in two ways: **in hand**, one item, visible to all and the tool or weapon of combat (D33); and **on it**, the rest, a shapeless bag for now. Capacity is in **kilograms** from the materials' masses (`content/movement.ron`: 20 kg to start), not in slots; the walk slows with the load. Acts are intentions within a tile's reach: pick up (into the empty hand, else carried), hold, stow, drop on one's tile, eat from the ground or from what is carried; every act wakes. The **server judges, the client proposes**: the client composes its menu from what it knows, the server refuses what cannot be done and says why. A dead body drops everything. Containers, loads carried by two, objects laid on a table or a stall fit the one-place rule without change.
- **Interface, first form** (decision 6): names on hover; a context menu on right-click (without dragging) on a tile within reach; an inventory panel listing the hand first, with masses and the load. Keys remain as shortcuts.


### D36 — Water as a volume: a river carries a flow that sets its width and depth
- **Reason**: a tile is about a metre (D31), so a tile of water half a metre deep is a half-cube, and a river is a number of half-cubes passing: its **flow**. One number then explains everything the banks need: two waters that meet become one, wider or deeper, never two rivers crossing; a river that parts around an island shares its flow between the arms; a ford is where a river spreads until it is half a metre deep right across, which only a small flow can do; a brook is the same water with a tiny flow. Width, depth, fords, islands, brooks and junctions stop being separate rules with separate dials, and later systems (the current, wading, drowning, boats, fishing, the sea at the end of the network) will read the same depth. It costs almost nothing: the flow is computed once at generation and the depth stored once per tile; nothing flows at every tick.
- **Consequences**:
  - The map keeps a **depth per tile in half-metres** (0 on land, 1 on a ford, up to 12 in a river: 6 m, a great river), regenerated with the terrain, not stored. Half a metre is crossed easily; a metre can be waded, slowly and without a load, when movement in water is built (a content cost per depth, not code); deeper, one swims or drowns.
  - The valley's waters form a **tree**: a main river from the cliff's foot to the south edge; **tributaries** from the edges and **brooks**, both counted by the map's side rather than its ground (8 November 2026): tributaries enter along the edges, and brooks are long and cross much of a map, so a larger map has them farther apart, not crowded. **22 to 34 tributaries and up to 41 to 65 brooks on 3072×3072** (about 35 to 50 find a place; 10 to 16 brooks on 768×768, where counted by the ground they had been 27 to 54, short and crowded). About two thirds of the brooks **enter by the map's edges** (west, east, south) or rise at the cliff's foot, and head into the valley (at least 30 tiles from that edge), never along an edge: past its first 8 steps a brook keeps 5 tiles from the map's edges and the cliff (along an edge, half of it lay past the ground drawn); the others rise where the ground is wet (a noise of wetness, like the groves; its average is a dial of the generator now and becomes a datum of each region once regions exist, D07) and at least 50 tiles from any water. Each runs to the nearest water, river or brook, **60 to 250 steps long**, never leaves the valley floor (out of the map or into the cliff and back would leave a piece alone), and keeps **25 tiles from the other brooks** except over the last stretch where it comes to join one. A brook **meanders** more than a river (7 November 2026): it holds a heading 2 to 5 steps (a river 4 to 10) and may stray up to 90 degrees from the heading it rose with (a river 67.5), still one step of 22.5 degrees at a time and never turning back. A river born of brooks that join keeps their winding course. Waters are **few** (7 November 2026): they are landmarks, not the landscape; three tributaries and eight to fourteen brooks crowded the valley, and the valley's water lies on **about 1.5 % of its ground** on average (9 November 2026; it was 4 to 5 %, then 3 %, then 2 %, as the project lead asked): fewer tributaries (7 to 12 on 3072 × 3072) and rivers **wider for the same flow** (a wide stretch about √(3.5 × flow) across, it was √(2 × flow), yet never under a metre deep outside a ford: too little water for that is a brook), so the land is cut less and walked more, most of it the rivers; one map alone ranges from 1.5 % to 2.5 %. Every water ends where it meets another; later, the main river reaches a sea.
  - The **section follows the flow**: `width × depth` covers the flow, a stretch being either *wide* (width about √(2 × flow)) or *deep* (about √flow), the choice drawn once per stretch between junctions. Water **one or two tiles wide is a brook** (`Terrain::Brook`, a metre deep at most, walked through); from three wide it is a river, up to 6 m deep; past that it only widens, **with no limit but its flow** (8 November 2026; it was 12 tiles and 4 m, and a river past 96 half-cubes simply lost the rest: on the 3072 valley the main river carries 125 to 224 at its mouth, now 17 to 23 tiles wide and 5 to 6 m deep). A limit of 64 tiles is only a safeguard. Realism first: a valley as wet as this one ends in a great river; drier regions, with fewer tributaries, will have thinner ones.
  - A **ford** is a lens where a river spreads to half a metre right across, so it is always wider than the river around it; only a flow of 12 half-cubes or less can do it, upstream of the junctions that make a river too strong. A river that could have a ford has one **by chance** (a dial, one in two to start), never by right: a ford is a place the terrain gives, rare enough to matter, and a bank without one is a reason for a bridge or a boat. Nothing is forced; a part of the map may wait for them. Only the starting ground is checked to be large enough.
  - A **divergence** splits a river in two arms around an island, from a rock to a large island; the smaller arm, when shallow enough, shoals into a ford, which is how one reaches the island.
  - **The banks breathe** (7 November 2026): a river with both banks parallel for tens of tiles looked drawn with a ruler, and a real bank is rarely straight for long: the two banks drift apart or together, or one bends alone and the river widens there. So each bank of a river, on its own, holds its line for 4 to 12 steps, then turns a quarter of a step of heading (about 5.6 degrees) out or in for 12 to 40 steps and comes back, so a river widens and narrows slowly (a whole step, 22.5 degrees, then half a step, made the river look misshapen: the lead's verdicts the same day); it stays between about a tile inside and six tiles outside its line (three until the lead asked for wider pools, the same day). Both out is a pool, both in a narrows, one out and one in the river sliding sideways, one alone a bend on one side. The width stays from 3 (never a brook) up to 18 for a **pool**, or half again the river's own width for a great river, and the **depth takes what the width leaves**, so the flow is kept: a narrows is deeper, a pool shallower, but **never less than a metre**. A pool wider than its flow can fill a metre deep is slower water holding more than its flow, as in a real basin, rather than a shoal: it never becomes a ford by accident (a ford stays a place chosen as such). The banks hold their lines near junctions, fords, divergences, the map's edges and on brooks, and a bank never bends within a step of a bend of the centre path, so a bank never turns by more than one step at once. The centre path also bends more often: a heading is held 4 to 10 steps (was 6 to 14).
  - The rules of the shore stay: straight runs in sixteen headings, a bend is a piece, a bank never cuts through a tile. The client draws the bank in the ground shader from the distance to the water (ShoreField), which follows any heading; the bank pieces, tiles and brushes tried before were retired on 9 November 2026 (code and images; the images are kept outside the repository). Brooks are drawn as a line on the ground.
  - **The water runs** (8 November 2026): every tile of water knows where it runs, the heading of the course that painted it, and how fast, from its section: `BASE_SPEED` (35 cm/s) times the flow over what the section holds, so a pool is slow, a narrows quick, and a ford, spread thin over its stones, half as fast again. Kept per tile with the map, regenerated, not stored; sent with each chunk (`currents`, protocol v11) so the client can make the water flow the right way at the right pace. The client cannot tell upstream from downstream from the tiles alone.
  - The client receives `Water`, `Ford` and `Brook` (protocol v9); depth reaches it when something is drawn from it. Every number here is a dial of the generator, to be tuned by looking at maps; the mechanism is the decision.

### D37 — Woods as a density: massifs, edges, clearings, copses, and species in stands
- **Reason**: the forest was one noise cut at one level: patches of one size scattered everywhere, a tile all forest or all grass, blind to rivers and cliff. A real valley has one or two great woods and open meadows, a wood thins out at its edge, opens in clearings, sends copses and lone trees into the meadow and lines its rivers. One number per tile, the **density of trees** (0 to 100), says all of it, the way the flow says the water (D36); and it is what will place the trees when they become things standing on the ground: many and close in the heart, a few at the edge, one alone in the meadow. Later it can also set how slowly one walks and how far one sees under the trees (a rule of play, not of shape: not yet).
- **Consequences** (decided 8 November 2026):
  - The map keeps per tile a **density of trees** and a **habitat** (meadow, edge, wood, heart, clearing, camp, copse, lone tree, riverside), regenerated with the terrain, not stored. A tile is `Forest` from a density of 35 up: the wood and the heart. The edge, copses' rims, lone and riverside trees stand on grass. The client still receives the terrain only; the protocol does not change.
  - **Massifs**: a broad noise (64 tiles) roughened by two finer ones (16 and 5 tiles), thickened where the ground is wet (the wetness the brooks rise from) and in a band ten rows deep at the cliff's foot. The level that makes a tile wooded is **chosen per map**, so the massifs, edges included, always cover **32 %** of the land: with one or two massifs a map, a fixed level gave anything from a third to three quarters.
  - **Edge, wood, heart**: the density rises from a massif's rim inwards over a few tiles; the heart (from 75) never touches the open meadow.
  - **Clearings**: ten a map in the woods, 3 to 7 tiles in radius, ragged; and a **camp clearing** 9 tiles around the place the first bodies start (`Map::camp`, tile 134, 139 of the first valley).
  - **Copses**: thirty a map in the meadow, 1.5 to 4 tiles in radius; **lone trees** on about 0.4 % of the meadow; **riverside trees** on the bank, two tiles from a river with a chance of 25 %, three tiles 15 % (8 November 2026: at one tile with 35 % they stood half in the water and were too many).
  - Over six maps the land is a third wooded (33 to 35 %), a fifth or so forest proper.
  - **Several species, grouped in stands that mix** (the lead's call): each habitat has its species (heart and wood: oak, pine, some birch; the band at the cliff's foot, its own habitat: pine; edge and clearings' rims: birch, hazel; riverside: willow; copses and lone trees: oak, wild apple, from `09-visual-production.md`). Each species also has its own **stands**, a noise about ten tiles across; a tree's species is drawn from the habitat's weights times the square of each species' stand there. Trees of one kind come in groups that mix at their borders: on the test maps about one neighbour in five is of another kind. The weights are content (`content/species.ron`), not code; balanced weights matter as much as the stands, since a species that outweighs the others everywhere leaves no room for groups of the others.
  - **The trees themselves** (8 November 2026): a tile holds a tree with a chance of 35 % of its density (a heart holds one tile in three or so), a lone or riverside tile always holds its tree, up to 30 cm from the tile's centre; nothing stands on a tile touching a river or a ford, even by a corner, since its trunk and crown would be drawn over the water. They are planted from the map and the content when the world is created and again when it is loaded, never stored; a felled tree will be stored when felling comes. A tree does not block the way for now: one walks under its crown, the forest's own cost slowing the walk. Chunks carry their trees and `world_state` the kinds of tree with their height (protocol v10). The client draws each tree as an image of its kind standing on its foot, sorted with the bodies from far to near so a body walks behind a trunk; which image of the kind and a size within 12 % are drawn from the tree's place, as with the bank pieces.
  - **The flora** (9 November 2026, the project lead's list `content/ai-reserve/notes/10-flora.md`): shrubs, plants and mushrooms in `content/flora.ron`, with the four trees still to come. Each kind has a **niche**: the places it grows (the habitats above, plus `bank` within two tiles of water, `stream` in a brook, `shallows` in a ford), the soil's moisture (from the distance to water and the valley's wetness) and the light (from the density of trees) it needs, and for some mushrooms a host tree, near it or on its trunk. Patches start where the niche holds (a single plant, a clump, a carpet, a fairy ring), with a chance set by its density times its **stand** (a smooth noise per kind: kinds come in places and mix where their stands meet) and the ground's **breath** (thick here, sparse a little further); each plant of a patch stands where its kind grows, and plants with volume keep their room. Placed **chunk by chunk when a chunk is asked for**, from the seed, nothing stored (D39); the view around the camp before the first connection. The server keeps the plants of the chunks asked for most recently only (a cache of `PLANT_CACHE_CHUNKS` chunks, several views' worth: 9 November 2026), so its memory no longer grows with the ground visited; a chunk forgotten is placed again, the same. A change to a plant (picked, trampled) must therefore be stored apart and applied on top, never only in that cache. What a plant does to whoever eats or touches it is hidden content (D30) and never here. One season for now: everything grows all year.
  - **Space** (9 November 2026, with the trees at their true height, D40): the massifs cover **24 %** of the land (it was 32 %) on a noise twice as broad (128 tiles), so the woods are fewer and larger and the plains wide; copses fewer but bigger (10 for every 256 × 256 tiles, 4 to 11 tiles in radius) and lone trees (0.09 % of the meadow) dot the plains; trees stand wider apart where the wood is thin (their room grows from the heart's to 1.6 times it), so an open wood shows gaps and a heart closes; riverside trees are about half as frequent, so the river and its banks show.
  - Every number here is a dial of the generator, to be tuned by looking at maps; the mechanism is the decision.

### D38 — Development tools only behind `--dev`
- **Reason**: the valley is 3 km across and meant to grow far more; looking at a place far away (a river's mouth, a brook, a wood) should not take half an hour of walking. But any shortcut a client can ask for is a cheat the day other people play, so it must not exist in play at all (D05: the server decides).
- **Consequences** (8 November 2026): the server takes `--dev`. Only then does it send, after `world_state`, a `dev_overview` (the whole map, coarse: one letter a cell of at least 8 tiles, 512 cells across at most whatever the map's size), and accept `dev_teleport` (protocol v12), which the simulation plays as an intention (`Intention::Teleport`: the walkable tile nearest the target, within 64 tiles, at once). Without `--dev` there is no overview and a teleport is ignored. On the client, M opens the overview turned as the game shows the map, a click teleports; Ctrl+click on the ground does the same nearby. Further development tools go behind the same flag.

### D39 — Performance by design: measured, budgeted, never at the cost of what is seen
- **Reason**: the world grows richer (trees, then 39 species of flora, D37 and `content/ai-reserve/notes/10-flora.md`) and larger (3 km across, meant for ten times and more). Optimisations bolted on afterwards are costly and risky; laid down early, most cost little and change nothing on screen. The project lead's rule: lighter, but never at the cost of the look or the game. Examined on 9 November 2026: the wind in the trees is computed by the graphics card (a few operations a pixel, no redraw on the processor) and is kept; F4 lets the project lead measure it.
- **Consequences** (9 November 2026):
  - **Measure first**: in `--dev` (D38), F3 shows what a frame costs (frames a second, the longest frame, script time, draw calls, canvas items, nodes, memory, chunks and trees held, chunks waiting to be built); F4 turns the wind off and on, to measure it. A fixed scene benchmarked at every pull request comes next, so a regression shows at once.
  - **Budget**: 60 frames a second at the worst place (a dense wood at the farthest zoom) on the project lead's machine; the world's share of a frame within about 4 ms of script time. Numbers to revise as the machine park becomes known.
  - **The techniques, in this order, each kept only if it changes nothing visible**: (1) images in **atlases** with one material, so trees and plants of every kind batch into a few draw calls even sorted by depth; (2) what is many and still drawn **in batches** (a multimesh a species and chunk), the sway staying in the shader; (3) chunks **built off the main thread** (Godot's worker threads), the main thread only showing the result; (4) a **compact protocol** for tiles and flora (a byte a tile, a species index and offset a plant: D26's "binary the day it matters" starts there); then, if the measures ask: (5) the still ground **baked** to images per chunk, at a resolution fitting the zoom; (6) **detail by zoom**: what would be under 2 or 3 pixels is not drawn; (7) the smallest ground flora painted by the ground shader from a density map rather than as objects.
  - **Measured on 9 November 2026** (the project lead's machine, a dense wood at the farthest zoom): about 5 fps at first; the see-through looked through every item for every tree under the mouse (fixed); then F5/F6 showed the trees' shader about 30 ms and the ground's 18 ms of a 52 ms frame; both lightened with nothing changed on screen, about 45 fps (22 ms: trees 8, ground 7, the rest 7). The trees' images are read a level sharper only up close, easing to the hardware's own level at zoom 0.5 and farther, where the eye cannot tell.
  - **Texture compression** (BPTC, best quality) was tested on the trees: four times less video memory (36 MB to 9), the contours a little softer only when enlarged. Kept in reserve, an import setting to turn on the day video memory becomes the limit, measured with F3.
  - **Amended on 9 November 2026: all art is compressed for the GPU, BC7 at best quality** (the lead's call after side-by-side comparisons, near and far: the loss is "very, very little" and the gain huge). It is the one exception to "nothing visible", and the line stays there: no lossier format (S3TC was visibly dirty), no smaller images without the lead's look. Measured by the light panel: the textures' video memory 159 MB to 43 (−73 %), their loading 1–1.7 s to 0.02 s, the packed download 46 MB to 26; on the 1:1 crops about 40 dB (37–42 by kind), no blocks, no shift of colour. Every tree has mipmaps (four had none). An image's last row holds its foot: the art tool keeps it nearly opaque so the compression's rounding never lifts a tree off the ground.
  - **Amended on 10 November 2026: one sharpness for every image** (the project lead's rule). Each image is made at the size it takes on the reference screen (3072 × 1920: 64 px a metre at zoom 1, stretched 1920 / 720 = 2.667 from the 1280 × 720 viewport) at its category's **reference zoom**: its kind's shown height (D40) × 64 × zoom × 2.667 px. Nothing is stored that the screen never shows, and nothing is softer than the rest. Plants and decorations are perfect at the closest zoom, 4 (683 px a shown metre: a chanterelle 55 px, a dandelion 137, an elder 1,707, where all were 384 before); trees will take zoom 2 to 2.5 when they are made again. `content/tools/flora_art.py` applies the rule (trees only with `--tree-zoom`; it used to size them by their true height, which would have made a 5,632 px oak) and lists every source smaller than its target, the only ones to make again: today 35 of the 39 decorations (kept as they are; the other 4, wider than their target, were reduced). Atlas images start on a 4-pixel block, 8 pixels apart, so no compression block or near mipmap mixes two plants; skyline packing fills it at 88 % (shelves: 79 %). Measured (BC7, mipmaps): the flora atlas 4096 × 2048 → 4096 × 5992, video memory 11.2 → 32.7 MB, zipped download 6.7 → 17.1 MB; the decorations 2.55 → 2.53 MB. The style reference image left the client's art (1.2 MB of package it never showed). The sharpness costs memory here, by choice: the rule fixes the look, the budget is watched with F3.
  - **The ground's cost must not grow with the number of terrains**: deserts, rock, snow, lakes, towns will come; their textures go in an array and a pixel blends only the two or three terrains that touch it, never all of them. Designed with the first new terrain, when it can be seen. Its cost follows the screen, never the map's size.
  - **The server stays proportional to what is used, not to the map**: the world is regenerated from its seed and only what changed is kept (a plant picked, a tree felled), placement is computed chunk by chunk when a chunk is sent, and path searches become hierarchical (D10, D11) as the map grows.

### D40 — One scale for the world: true under 3 m, eased above
- **Reason**: the project lead's call (9 November 2026): the world keeps true proportions, as far as the screen allows. Each thing had had its own scale (the body a third of a person, trees a fifth of a real tree, plants at their true height), and reeds stood twice the body's height.
- **Consequences**: everything under 3 m is drawn at its true height (a body 1.7 m, a reed 2 m, a dandelion 20 cm, a mushroom 8 cm); above, heights ease towards reality without reaching it, 3 m × (h / 3 m)^0.5 (a 22 m oak shows about 8 m, nearly five times a body). ^0.7 (about 12 m) then ^0.6 (about 10 m) were tried on 9 November 2026: the trees made the land look cramped at the farthest zoom; smaller trees give the sense of space the project lead asked for, so tall things stay tall without filling the screen. One function on the client (`Scale.shown`); content gives true heights. Small plants are drawn a little brighter so the grass does not swallow them. The trees move to their true heights with this rule in a following step, with their spacing, since bigger crowns mean fewer trees and cost more to draw (D39).
