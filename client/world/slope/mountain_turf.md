# The mountain turf — a family of five tiles

The grass the slopes are drawn with (D44, `ground.gdshader`, `slope_lit`): the lead's mountain grass, drawn by our own renderer, as a **family of five interchangeable tiles** of the same grass. Each tile has its own pattern of blades; all five share one border band, so any tile lays beside any other, in any order, without a seam. The ground shader lays one tile a cell of the turf's repetition, chosen by a hash of the cell (`slope_turf_variant`), so the slopes read as one uniform grass, not as a picture repeated.

- **Files**: `mountain_turf_1.png` … `mountain_turf_5.png`, 1536 × 1536 each, RGB. One tile is 384 px on the screen at zoom 1 (`SLOPE_LOOK["slope_turf_px"]`), so at the maximum zoom (2.5) the tile still gives 1.6 texture pixels a screen pixel: the blades stay crisp where 1024 px blurred them. 2048 px looked no better in play and cost twice the video memory (80 MB for the five with mipmaps, against 45 MB at 1536). The look's tint inverse is already baked into the pixels, so the shader's `slope_tint` lays on them as it did on the single tile before.
- **How they are read**: `ChunkView.slope_turf_set` builds one `Texture2DArray` from the five at load, one layer a tile, with mipmaps, sampled `filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic` (the slopes are seen at a grazing angle once the texture is squeezed along the slope; anisotropic filtering keeps the blades sharp there); the array is laid out as `angles × variants` (one row of angles today) so a second index, the slope's steepness, can choose the row later without the layout changing. The developer overlay's key G lays tile 1 alone, to see what the family brings against one tile repeated.
- **Made**: 16 November 2026 by our renderer `cretes-vagues.py` (in the art reserve `content/ai-reserve/notes/calibration-pentes/`, git-ignored; to be moved into the repository later), the five with one dial set and seeds 1 to 5, drawn at 1024-scale with ×4 supersampling and delivered at 1536: `--out-side 1536 --cream-none 1.0 --cream-a 0 --masses 0 --fur 0 --fat 0 --thin 1.5 --dark-w 1.15 --wedge-l 0.85 --shade-w 0 --shade-a 0 --fringes 0.2 --bed-w 8 --bed-a 0.5 --ss 4 --spike 40 --cream-len 135 --band-jitter 12 --band-drift 10 --field-scale 420 --band-len 100 --blend 96 --family 7 --margin 32 --seed 1..5`, with `--game` writing the tile divided by the screen tint. In words: no cream patches, no fur masses, no fat flames; blades 1.5× longer and wider than the first family, their dark upper edge 1.15× wider, their lit lower edge 15 % darker; the crests (the diagonal lines of blades rising like foam) jittered and drifted by a slow random field so no line repeats across the tile; the five tiles share one 32 px border band blended over 96 px.
- **Status**: validated by the project lead on 16 November 2026 ("c'est parfait comme ça"), at every zoom down to the maximum, on the massif and the vivarium worlds. What remains for the slopes is a second row of the array per steepness (the angle set) and, later, the blades' direction.
