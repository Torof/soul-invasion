# The mountain turf — a family of five tiles

The grass the slopes are drawn with (D44, `ground.gdshader`, `slope_lit`): the lead's mountain grass, drawn by our own renderer, as **four rows of five interchangeable tiles** of the same grass, one row a crest angle. In a row each tile has its own pattern of blades and all five share one border band, so any tile of the row lays beside any other, in any order, without a seam. The ground shader lays one tile a cell of the turf's repetition: the row from the cell's steepness (`slope_turf_row`), the tile from a hash of the cell (`slope_turf_variant`), so the slopes read as one uniform grass that climbs harder where the ground does, not as a picture repeated.

- **Files**: `mountain_turf_<angle>_<n>.png`, angle 20, 32, 43 or 55 (the crests' climb, degrees above the horizontal) and n 1 to 5, twenty tiles of 1536 × 1536, RGB, imported **VRAM compressed (BPTC) with their mipmaps** (3.1 MB each in video memory instead of 12.6 uncompressed; the twenty 63 MB). One tile is 384 px on the screen at zoom 1 (`SLOPE_LOOK["slope_turf_px"]`), so at the maximum zoom (2.5) the tile still gives 1.6 texture pixels a screen pixel: the blades stay crisp where 1024 px blurred them. 2048 px looked no better in play and cost twice the video memory (80 MB for the five with mipmaps, against 45 MB at 1536). The look's tint inverse is already baked into the pixels, so the shader's `slope_tint` lays on them as it did on the single tile before.
- **How they are read**: `ChunkView.slope_turf_set` builds one `Texture2DArray` from the five at load, one layer a tile, with mipmaps, sampled `filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic` (the slopes are seen at a grazing angle once the texture is squeezed along the slope; anisotropic filtering keeps the blades sharp there); the array is laid out as `angles × variants`, layer = angle × 5 + variant. **The row**: the steepness at the cell's middle, in metres a tile, gives the crest's angle `slope_angle_first + slope_angle_per_metre × rise` degrees, bounded to `slope_angle_last` (20 + 60 × rise, to 55, by default), and the nearest of the four rows is laid; the lead's choice of 16 November 2026 (four angles from 20 to 55, "plus de variations": on a slope that steepens the rows change from cell to cell, twenty tiles mingling instead of five, and at 55 the blades still stand off the crest line, where at 70 they lay along it). `slope_angle_per_metre` is his dial: the developer overlay's Page Up and Page Down, Backspace back to the look's; what he keeps goes into `SLOPE_LOOK` by hand. The developer overlay's key G lays tile 1 alone, to see what the family brings against one tile repeated.
- **Made**: 16 November 2026 by our renderer `cretes-vagues.py` (in the art reserve `content/ai-reserve/notes/calibration-pentes/`, git-ignored; to be moved into the repository later), the twenty with one dial set, `--angle` 20, 32, 43 or 55 and seeds 1 to 5, drawn at 1024-scale with ×4 supersampling and delivered at 1536: `--angle A --out-side 1536 --cream-none 1.0 --cream-a 0 --masses 0 --fur 0 --fat 0 --thin 1.5 --dark-w 1.15 --wedge-l 0.85 --shade-w 0 --shade-a 0 --fringes 0.2 --bed-w 8 --bed-a 0.5 --ss 4 --spike 40 --cream-len 135 --band-jitter 12 --band-drift 10 --field-scale 420 --band-len 100 --blend 96 --family 7 --margin 32 --seed 1..5`, with `--game` writing the tile divided by the screen tint. In words: no cream patches, no fur masses, no fat flames; blades 1.5× longer and wider than the first family, their dark upper edge 1.15× wider, their lit lower edge 15 % darker; the crests (the diagonal lines of blades rising like foam) jittered and drifted by a slow random field so no line repeats across the tile; the five tiles of a row share one 32 px border band blended over 96 px (the band is drawn at the row's angle, so a cell whose row differs from its neighbour's meets it at a seam: the crests break by about 12 degrees there, where the picture changes anyway).
- **Status**: the grass validated by the project lead on 16 November 2026 ("c'est parfait comme ça"), at every zoom down to the maximum, on the massif and the vivarium worlds; the four angles chosen the same day on a sheet of 20, 45 and 70 degrees (`content/ai-reserve/review/pentes/v18-angles/planche-angles.png`). What remains for the slopes is the dial's value, set by him in play, and, later, the blades' direction.
